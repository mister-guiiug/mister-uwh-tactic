# Explain an underwater hockey play

In underwater hockey, the game happens on the bottom, breathing at the surface, and the two alternate all the time. A play is hard to explain out loud: you have to say where everyone is, but also at what depth, and when they go back down. A tactics board makes it visible.

## Start from a position everyone knows

The start of play is the only line-up the rules impose: each team against its end wall, the puck in the centre. In Mister UWH Tactic it is the position of a new board, and the rule situations also place an equal puck, an advantage puck or a penalty shot as the international rules describe them.

## Line a team up

A line-up reads front to back: in 3-2-1, three players up front near the halfway line, two in the middle, one back in front of the goal. The most common are 3-2-1, 2-3-1, 2-2-2, 1-2-1-2, 2-1-3 and 1-2-3. The board places one in a single gesture, in the team's own half, and can name the positions.

## Break it into short steps

A step is a moment of the play. A new step starts as a copy of the previous one, and you only move the players who move. The ghost of the previous step shows who moved, and by how much. Arrows show a run, a pass along the bottom, or a flick that lifts the puck.

## Say who is at the surface, and for how long

For each player and each step, choose "at the surface" or "underwater", and give each step its real duration. The breath-hold timeline adds up each player's consecutive seconds on the bottom and flags anyone beyond the limit: that is where a play falls apart, when a player who must breathe leaves a hole on the bottom.

## Place the referees and the substitutes

A team has six players in the water and four substitutes, who come on and off through their substitution area along the side. Officiating takes three referees in the water and one on deck. The water referees' field of view, shown as an option, reveals what they cannot see.

## Replay, watch in 3D, take it with you

Playback runs the steps as an animation. The 3D view shows the same play in the pool at its real depth: from the stands, underwater, or through the eyes of a player or a referee. Then print one thumbnail per step, record a video from the 3D view, or show a QR code: the link carries the whole play, and opens without an account.
